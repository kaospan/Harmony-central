# Music Theory Reference

## Roman Numeral Chord Notation

### Major Key Progressions

In a major key, Roman numerals indicate the scale degree and chord quality:

- **I** - Tonic major (stable, home)
- **ii** - Supertonic minor
- **iii** - Mediant minor
- **IV** - Subdominant major (moves toward tonic)
- **V** - Dominant major (strong pull to tonic)
- **vi** - Submediant minor
- **vii°** - Leading tone diminished

Example in C major:
- I = C major
- ii = D minor
- iii = E minor
- IV = F major
- V = G major
- vi = A minor
- vii° = B diminished

### Minor Key Progressions

In a minor key:

- **i** - Tonic minor
- **ii°** - Supertonic diminished
- **III** - Mediant major
- **iv** - Subdominant minor
- **v/V** - Dominant minor/major
- **VI** - Submediant major
- **VII** - Subtonic major

Example in A minor:
- i = A minor
- ii° = B diminished
- III = C major
- iv = D minor
- V = E major
- VI = F major
- VII = G major

## Chord Extensions

### 7th Chords

- **V7** - Dominant 7th (adds 7th scale degree)
- **maj7** - Major 7th
- **m7** - Minor 7th
- **dim7** - Diminished 7th
- **m7b5** - Half-diminished 7th

## Common Chord Progressions

### The "Axis" Progression: I-V-vi-IV

One of the most popular progressions in modern pop music:

**Examples:**
- "Let It Be" - The Beatles
- "Don't Stop Believin'" - Journey
- "Someone Like You" - Adele
- "Africa" - Toto
- "No Woman, No Cry" - Bob Marley

**Why it works:**
1. I (tonic) - establishes home
2. V (dominant) - creates tension
3. vi (relative minor) - adds emotion
4. IV (subdominant) - prepares return to I

### Other Popular Progressions

**I-IV-V (Three-chord progression)**
- Classic rock and blues
- Examples: "Wild Thing", "La Bamba"

**I-vi-IV-V (50s progression)**
- Doo-wop and early rock
- Examples: "Stand by Me", "Every Breath You Take"

**vi-IV-I-V (Sensitive progression)**
- Emotional, introspective
- Examples: "Grenade" - Bruno Mars

**ii-V-I (Jazz progression)**
- Standard in jazz
- Creates smooth voice leading

## Cadences

### Authentic Cadence (V-I)

The strongest, most conclusive cadence.

**Types:**
- **Perfect Authentic**: V-I with both chords in root position, tonic in soprano
- **Imperfect Authentic**: V-I but with inversions or other notes in soprano

**Function:** Creates strong sense of resolution and conclusion

**Examples:**
- End of most classical pieces
- Chorus endings in pop songs

### Plagal Cadence (IV-I)

Also known as the "Amen cadence."

**Function:** Gentler resolution than authentic cadence

**Examples:**
- "Amen" at the end of hymns
- Final chords in many Beatles songs

### Deceptive Cadence (V-vi)

Expected resolution to I, but goes to vi instead.

**Function:** Delays resolution, adds surprise and emotion

**Examples:**
- "Let Down" - Radiohead
- Various points in "Bohemian Rhapsody"

### Half Cadence (x-V)

Ends on the dominant chord, creating tension.

**Function:** Incomplete, asks for continuation

**Examples:**
- Middle of verses
- Question-answer phrase patterns

## Functional Harmony

### Three Main Functions

1. **Tonic (I, iii, vi)** - Stable, at rest
2. **Subdominant (ii, IV)** - Moves away from tonic
3. **Dominant (V, vii°)** - Strong pull back to tonic

### Common Progressions by Function

**T → S → D → T** (Tonic → Subdominant → Dominant → Tonic)
- Most common harmonic movement
- Example: I → IV → V → I

**T → D → T** (Direct to dominant)
- Example: I → V → I

## Key Relationships

### Relative Major/Minor

Share the same key signature:

- C major ↔ A minor
- G major ↔ E minor
- D major ↔ B minor
- A major ↔ F# minor
- E major ↔ C# minor
- B major ↔ G# minor
- F major ↔ D minor
- Bb major ↔ G minor
- Eb major ↔ C minor

**Musical Relationship:** Same notes, different tonal centers

### Parallel Major/Minor

Same tonic note, different quality:

- C major ↔ C minor
- G major ↔ G minor
- D major ↔ D minor

**Musical Relationship:** Different notes (altered 3rd, 6th, 7th)

## Modal Interchange

Borrowing chords from parallel minor in major key (or vice versa):

**In C major, can borrow from C minor:**
- bIII (Eb major)
- iv (F minor)
- bVI (Ab major)
- bVII (Bb major)

**Effect:** Adds darker, more complex harmony

**Examples:**
- "Creep" - Radiohead (uses bIII and bVII)
- Many Beatles songs

## Chord Inversions

### Notation

- Root position: no marking (e.g., I)
- First inversion: /3 (e.g., I/3 or I6)
- Second inversion: /5 (e.g., I/5 or I64)

### Voice Leading

Inversions create smoother bass lines:

**Instead of:** I - IV - V - I (C - F - G - C)

**With inversions:** I - IV/3 - V - I (C - F/A - G - C)

Bass line: C - A - G - C (smoother, more melodic)

## Analyzing Similarity

### How Harmony Central Compares Songs

1. **Progression Similarity (50% weight)**
   - Exact chord matches
   - Functional equivalents (I and iii both tonic)
   - Edit distance algorithm

2. **Cadence Similarity (30% weight)**
   - Same cadence types present
   - Position in progression

3. **Structural Similarity (20% weight)**
   - Same key or relative keys
   - Same time signature
   - Similar progression length

### Similarity Score Interpretation

- **0.9-1.0**: Very similar (likely same progression)
- **0.7-0.9**: Similar (shared patterns, functions)
- **0.5-0.7**: Somewhat similar (some common elements)
- **0.3-0.5**: Loosely similar (functional relationships)
- **0.0-0.3**: Different (little in common)
